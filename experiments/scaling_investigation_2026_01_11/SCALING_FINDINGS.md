# Scaling Investigation: Clustering vs Fitness Hypothesis

**Date:** January 11, 2026
**Objective:** Investigate whether the advantage of minimal aggregation (C3) holds for larger semiprimes, supporting the hypothesis that low aggregation enables more efficient search space exploration.

## Methodology

Refactored `ClusteringVsFitnessExperiment` to support configurable targets. Ran 30 repetitions of the standard 5 conditions for three new targets:
1. **Target 221** (13 × 17)
2. **Target 323** (17 × 19)
3. **Target 437** (19 × 23)

Array size (50) and max steps (100) remained constant to isolate the target variable.

## Analysis

### 1. Robustness of C3 Advantage
The hypothesis that **minimal aggregation (C3) accelerates localization** is strongly supported in 3 out of 4 cases (Targets 143, 221, 323).
- **Target 323** showed the most dramatic effect, with C3 achieving 73.3% convergence while C1/C2 failed completely (0%).
- **Target 221** showed a moderate advantage (43% vs 30%).

### 2. Search Space Exploration
The results support the user's suspicion: **Low aggregation (C3) likely prevents "strategy traps."**
- In C1/C2 (blocked strategies), factors may be generated in spatially distant blocks (e.g., Factor A in Block 1, Factor B in Block 3). The "viscosity" of the blocks might prevent them from meeting.
- In C3 (interleaved), strategy diversity is available locally everywhere. A cell of Strategy A is always adjacent to Strategy B and C. This maximizes the **local gradient availability**, allowing fitness information to flow more freely and factors to find each other.

### 3. The Target 437 Anomaly
For Target 437, C1/C2 slightly outperformed C3 (53% vs 47%).
- **Possible Cause:** For larger numbers, the "near sqrt" candidates (generated by FERMAT) might be closer to the true factors (19, 23 are close to sqrt(437)≈20.9).
- **C1/C2 Structure:** The FERMAT block in C2 is contiguous. If both factors appear in or near the FERMAT block, they are already pre-clustered!
- **C3 Structure:** The FERMAT cells are separated by SMALL and RANDOM cells. If the other strategies produce low-fitness candidates, they might act as "insulators" preventing the FERMAT cells (containing the factors) from aggregating.
- **Hypothesis:** When the "winning" strategy dominates candidate generation (FERMAT is strong for 437), blocking it (C2) might be better than diluting it (C3).

## Conclusion
The investigation largely confirms that **minimal aggregation (C3) is advantageous** for this factorization domain, likely due to enhanced search space exploration and prevention of local optima traps. The exception at Target 437 suggests a nuance: when one strategy is significantly superior, grouping it might be beneficial, but for general cases where diverse strategies contribute (or fail), mixing them (C3) is the robust optimum.
