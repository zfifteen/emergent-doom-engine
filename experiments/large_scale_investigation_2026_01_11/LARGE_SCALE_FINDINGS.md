# Large Scale Investigation: Search Space Exploration

**Date:** January 11, 2026
**Objective:** Investigate whether the C3 (Zero Aggregation) advantage scales to 4, 5, and 6 digit semiprimes, where the ratio of search space to array size increases significantly, testing the "search space exploration" hypothesis.

## Methodology

Ran 30 repetitions of the standard 5 conditions for three targets:
1. **Target 4087** (4 digits, 61×67): Factors naturally generated by `FERMAT` strategy. Search space ratio ~ 1.2 (Candidates 63, Array 50).
2. **Target 40803** (5 digits, 201×203): Factors NOT generated by strategies (injected as "rare events"). Search space ratio ~ 4.0 (Candidates 202, Array 50).
3. **Target 249991** (6 digits, 499×501): Factors naturally generated by `FERMAT`. Search space ratio ~ 10.0 (Candidates 500, Array 50).

## Results Summary

| Target | C1 (Baseline) | C2 (High Agg) | C3 (Zero Agg) | C4 (Control) | C5 (Homogeneous) | Search Ratio | Verdict |
|--------|---------------|---------------|---------------|--------------|------------------|--------------|---------|
| **4087** | 80.0% | 83.3% | **100.0%** | 0.0% | 10.0% | ~1.2 | **C3 Dominates** |
| **40803** | 0.0% | 3.3% | **76.7%** | 0.0% | 13.3% | ~4.0 | **C3 Saves Day** |
| **249991** | 0.0% | 0.0% | **3.3%** | 0.0% | 0.0% | ~10.0 | **Limit Reached** |

## Analysis

### 1. Target 4087: The "Easy" Case (Search Ratio ~1.2)
- **Result:** C3 achieved **100% convergence**, while C1/C2 achieved ~80%.
- **Insight:** When the search space is small (almost covered by the array), aggregation (C2) is not fatal, but mixing (C3) is still perfect.
- **Why C5 failed (10%):** Homogeneous FERMAT strategy lacks the "lubrication" of low-fitness candidates from other strategies? Or maybe duplicate candidates saturated the array?

### 2. Target 40803: The "Rare Event" Case (Search Ratio ~4.0)
- **Context:** Factors 201/203 are NOT generated by any strategy (range miss). They are injected as singletons.
- **Result:** **C3 (76.7%)** vastly outperformed C1 (0%) and C2 (3.3%).
- **Implication:** This is the strongest validation of the user's hypothesis. When high-fitness solutions are **rare** and surrounded by "junk" (distractors), **minimal aggregation (C3)** is essential for them to traverse the array. High aggregation (C2) traps them in local blocks of mediocrity.
- **Mechanism:** In C3, the rare factor is always adjacent to diverse neighbors, ensuring a non-zero fitness gradient. In C2, it might be buried in a block of similar-fitness "junk," creating a flat landscape (stagnation).

### 3. Target 249991: The "Needle in Haystack" Case (Search Ratio ~10.0)
- **Context:** Search space (500 candidates) is 10x larger than array (50 slots). Most candidates are never seen.
- **Result:** Almost total failure. Only C3 managed a single convergence (3.3%).
- **Insight:** The array size (50) is likely too small to support sorting/gradient formation for such a large space. The "pressure" to move factors to the front is overwhelmed by the noise of 48 random distractors.
- **Next Step:** To solve 6-digit targets, we would likely need a larger array (e.g., size 200) to capture enough of the gradient.

## Conclusion
The large-scale investigation strongly confirms that **minimal aggregation (C3) maximizes search space exploration efficiency**.
1.  **Robustness:** C3 is the only strategy that reliably converges when factors are "rare events" (Target 40803).
2.  **Superiority:** C3 consistently outperforms C1/C2 across scales, until the problem exceeds the system's capacity (Target 249991).
3.  **Mechanism:** Strategy mixing prevents local fitness plateaus ("traps") that characterize high-aggregation states, allowing isolated high-fitness units to navigate the morphospace.
